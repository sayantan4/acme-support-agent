Acme Invest email support · prompt history

What changed in each prompt version, and why

Two prompts drive the orchestrator: the scope gate (gpt-5.4-nano, decides who handles a message) and the main agent (gpt-5.4-mini, SOPs + tools + reply). Every change bumps a version string, so eval results can be traced to the exact prompt. Diffs below are the actual edits, taken from the change history.

Trade-off and what's left
  • Turned-away customers fell from 23 to 3, at the cost of catching fewer out-of-scope emails (64.5% to 56.6%). A missed out-of-scope email only costs one agent run, so this is the right direction.
  • Still turned away: "If I only have 1 other US card, can you take it?", "show me how to top up with my card", and "Acme Invest has decided to end their relationship with me…".

Result

Why it was reverted
  • It fixed all three targeted emails, but 18 out-of-scope emails that v3 caught were now sent to the main agent.
  • The cause was the new in-scope line "money lost to a scam that went to or from Acme Invest". The nano model matched on "scam" and "money lost" and ignored the condition.

Small models follow keywords, not conditions. Keep the in-scope list to plain topics and put exclusions in the "does not offer" list. Between v3 and v4, decisions also flipped in the direction the change didn't target, partly run-to-run noise from the model; comparing versions over 2–3 runs each would separate the two.

Not built: scope-v5 would be v4 without the scam line. The prompt was frozen at v3 instead.

agent-v1
superseded
2 Oct 2026
What it did
  • Follow the SOP's debugging steps in order, calling the tools they name; use only facts from the messages, SOPs and tool results.
  • Escalate if the SOP says so, no SOP section fits, or a tool fails in a way the SOP doesn't cover.
  • Answer as JSON: summary, reply, escalate, escalation_note, sop_ids_used. No verified customer means summary only.
Problems found in real threads
  • It replied to "Thanks" with a full re-check of the deposit ("I checked your deposit again, it is still pending…").
  • On a lawyer follow-up it sent the same holding reply again (89% similar to the first).
  • Gmail's quoted history ("On … wrote: > …") looked like new content, so it re-answered old questions.
  • It never saw what we had already replied, because C1 only fetched the customer's messages.
Baseline

Three of the five failures were expectations written too strictly, not bad replies. The two real failures were the two above.
